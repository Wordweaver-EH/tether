# Cover uncertainty v2: sparse checking, no demonstrated recovery benefit

**The frozen study did not establish a useful-checking or recovery benefit.** Calibrated monitoring became sparse, known reset jumps were separated from prediction assessments, and checks causally changed gaze. These engineering gains did not improve the primary clean-recovery endpoint or return-to-routine outcome. Small score advantages remain descriptive, heterogeneous secondary findings.

All 48 predetermined bouts completed after explicit release against published checkpoint [59a62eeb690ab8ffc3478ff688674bfbea11f84c](https://github.com/Wordweaver-EH/tether/commit/59a62eeb690ab8ffc3478ff688674bfbea11f84c). No source, model, threshold, opponent, endpoint, seed or matrix size changed after scoring began. No cell was excluded or rerun.

## Fixed study and primary outcome

Four seed clusters × two subject seats × familiar/changed route × full/monitor-off/count-yoked scheduled produced 48 sixty-second bouts. Seats are repeated measurements within a seed, not independent replications. Ordinary Cover geometry, scoring, weapons, hit resets, 150 ms sensor delay, 30 Hz decisions and seeded motor-noise sampling remained active.

Every condition/arm has 24 fixed post-boundary excursion opportunities: clocks 31, 43 and 55 seconds in eight bouts. A clean recovery requires the same circuit to complete, an actual cover-occluded hidden sample, subsequent observed return and low residual assessment, all before its bounded horizon and without an intervening reset. Not-started, reset-interrupted, unresolved and terminal-censored opportunities remain in the denominator. The final window ends with the bout; this is a bounded mission outcome, not an uncensored recovery-time estimate.

Full minus off changes the primary changed-route recovery proportion by **−4.17 percentage points**; full minus scheduled is **zero**. In seed order 5107, 6211, 7307, 8423, full-minus-off differences in successful recoveries per bout, averaging seats, are **−0.5, 0, +1, −1**. Against scheduled they are **+0.5, 0, −0.5, 0**. Four heterogeneous seed clusters establish neither a general benefit nor a strong harm claim.

The switch-minus-familiar differences in recovery effects are +4.17 percentage points against off and +12.5 against scheduled. These arise because full did worse in familiar windows; they are not positive changed-route recovery effects. The primary result is not rescued by that interaction.

The routine endpoint requires a clean recovered circuit followed by an observed uninterrupted second without delivered checks, with Objective focus present and no reset. It is **return to a routine**, not a learned habit: tactical action-policy learning remained disabled.

Completed-only changed-route recovery medians were 3.70 s full, 3.93 s off and 3.90 s scheduled, among only 9, 10 and 9 clean completions. These selected latencies do not establish faster unconditional recovery.

## The gameplay demand occurred, with unequal exposure retained

True cover disappearance survived active combat. After the boundary, full, off and scheduled started 21, 18 and 17 of 24 changed-route opportunities; 15, 15 and 13 opportunities respectively included actual cover blocking. Their opportunity outcomes were:

- Full: 10 returned circuits, 7 reset interruptions, 4 terminal-censored and 3 not started. One physically returned circuit had no observed target return before the horizon, leaving 9 primary successes.
- Off: 11 returned, 4 reset interruptions, 3 terminal-censored and 6 not started; one physically returned circuit likewise lacked an observed target return before the horizon, leaving 10 primary successes.
- Scheduled: 9 returned, 5 reset interruptions, 3 terminal-censored and 7 not started; 9 primary successes.

These are different closed-loop trajectories, not matched surprise events. Hitting an opponent and interrupting its route remains legitimate gameplay, but it cannot demonstrate recovery from an unexperienced route change. No exposure failure was discarded.

The earlier development waypoint deadlock and its minimal measured-or-predicted-arrival repair are documented in [PILOT.md](PILOT.md). The failed pilot remains retained; no further repair or tuning followed held-out results.

## Checking became sparse; its usefulness was not established

Full delivered **132 pulses in 28,736 decisions (0.46%)**: 63 familiar and 69 changed-route. Individual bouts had 1–16 pulses. It did not retain the earlier nearly continuous high-error control loop.

However, **115/132 full pulses occurred while the opponent was already visible**. Only 17 began with a missing opponent, and 9 met the narrow proxy “fresh observation within one sensor-second, without reset.” Scheduled achieved that proxy on **32/131** delivered checks. In changed-route bouts alone the counts were **5/69 full versus 21/69 scheduled**. Already-visible checks can still affect control, but these data do not demonstrate superior contingent information gathering.

The proxy is an association on different trajectories, not causal information gain. Monitor-off retains ordinary attention and planning; zero added checks is not zero observation or cognition.

All 16 scheduled pairs exactly matched full's **proposed** pulse count, using the frozen independent-phase spacing formula. Scheduled delivered 131 of 132 proposals; full delivered all 132. The sole discrepancy was seed 5107/P2/familiar, 16 versus 15 delivered requests, within the preregistered tolerance. No pair failed the delivery-matching tolerance, but realized information and computation were not matched.

Count yoking receives full's post-intervention whole-bout count. It is a research comparator, not a deployable policy or a pure equal-compute test of timing. Its familiar/switch schedules can differ even before the switch. This limitation was specified before scoring.

## Real attention actuation, limited causal scope

Independent comparisons of original logs identify a first full/off command divergence in all 16 seed/seat/condition pairs with identical prior commands and identical current received percepts. Each first divergence is a delivered calibrated pulse changing **gaze only**, with movement and weapon commands unchanged. For example, seed 5107/P1/familiar first diverges at sensor time 9.366667 s after a fresh calibrated-error pulse.

Common-percept replay through full/off reproduces all **28,736 full desired commands**. It yields **263 differing gaze commands** in total, no movement or weapon differences. Later differences are responses to a shared externally supplied sensor stream, including motor-noise consequences; they are not independent counterfactual gameplay outcomes or proof that each check caused new information.

## Reset separation and empirical calibration

Across 48 bouts there were **427 actual resets and 5,320 calibrated assessments**. Observed-reset counts matched those 427 actual resets in these runs, and **zero calibrated assessments straddled an actual reset**. Actual reset truth was used only for auditing; the policy used its own impossible spawn displacement plus public score change. This successful sample does not prove every possible near-spawn reset is identifiable.

Full had 856/920 familiar and 762/830 changed-route assessments within the frozen residual-rate threshold. Changed-route full horizon bins were 701/766 within threshold for 0.3–0.4 s, 52/55 for 0.4–0.7 s and 9/9 for 0.7–1 s. These serial, on-policy samples and small long-horizon denominators are empirical diagnostics, not calibrated probability guarantees or matched counterfactual estimator quality.

The trained velocity coefficient and threshold remained frozen. The completed-absence deadline remains a censored-sample heuristic; long-stale attention targets extrapolate beyond the calibration horizon. No improved general belief estimator is established.

This bounded milestone ends with a reusable trained predictor/checking component and a negative primary result. It does not demonstrate acquired action habits, trained second-domain transfer, broad generality, consciousness or a useful C2 recovery advantage. Further work should first explain and improve the value of checking under ordinary play in a new, separately trained and frozen protocol; these held-out results must not be repurposed for tuning and relabeled as fresh evidence.
